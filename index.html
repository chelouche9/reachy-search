🔎 Reachy Search

Say “hey Reachy”. Hold something up. Ask.

Reachy looks at the object, drums its antennas while it searches the web, and tells you the answer out loud.

How it works

  1. Say “hey Reachy”

    A local Whisper model listens for its name. It perks up and asks what it can do for you. Booping an antenna works too.

  2. Ask out loud

    It grabs a camera frame as you finish talking. Speech recognition runs locally and your microphone audio never leaves the machine. After it answers, it stays awake for a minute, so follow-up questions need no wake word.

  3. It thinks out loud

    A Claude agent sees the frame and decides for itself whether to search the web with Tavily. When it does, Reachy says “Searching…” and drums its antennas until the answer is in.

  4. It answers

    Spoken through the robot's own speaker, with the head moving along to the voice.

“What is this?”

That's a stovetop moka pot. Water boils in the bottom chamber and pressure pushes it up through the coffee. Yours looks like a six-cup aluminium one.

Ask it anything you'd ask while holding the thing: is this dishwasher safe, find me a cheaper one, what do I even do with this.

The thinking move

The latency isn't hidden. The animation is the interface.

drumroll

Head tips forward and the antennas drum an impatient alternating rhythm, like fingers on a desk, with the head cocking to a new angle every bar.

the aha

When the answer lands, it pops up to face you before it speaks. A search that took five seconds ends with a visible “found it”.

built on real timing

Beat structure, holds and asymmetry were measured from Pollen's animator-recorded emotion moves, so it reads as thinking rather than as a screensaver.

Setup

Open your Reachy Mini dashboard, find Reachy Search in the app store and click Install. Then open the app's settings page and paste in two API keys, both with usable free tiers. That's the whole configuration. Full setup and the code are on GitHub.

  • Anthropic: sees the object, writes the answer
  • Tavily: searches the web

Speech recognition and synthesis are local, so there's nothing else to sign up for.
